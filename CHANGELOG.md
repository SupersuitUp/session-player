# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Distributed from GitHub, no longer published to npm** (Gary, 2026-10-28: "Why do we need session player on npm at all remove it"). Its only installers are the two hosts that serve it, continental-works-site and supersuit-wiki, and they now pin a commit of this repo (`"@supersuit/session-player": "github:SupersuitUp/session-player#<sha>"`) and run their `--player-only` sync as before. `package.json` is `"private": true` so nothing can publish it by accident, the npm publish workflow (`publish.yml`, OIDC trusted publishing) is gone, and `ci.yml` runs the same tests and install smoke on every push and pull request instead. A player change ships by landing on `main`; there is no version bump, tag or publish. Versions up to 0.11.0 stay on npm and receive nothing newer. `test/package-name.test.mjs` asserts the package is private and that no workflow publishes it.
- **On a real phone the session clock no longer covers the title** (SupersuitUp/session-player#2, seen 2026-10-28 at 390x844 in 'Auto · this phone'). With no drawn device the clock pill sat in the Claude-skin header, whose title is centred absolutely, so '9:03' was drawn over the session title and its 'MacBook Pro' line. It now sits in the counter above the screen, beside "N of M steps", where the real app draws nothing; the MacBook window (whole or at phone width) keeps it in its header and the drawn iPhone in its status bar, unchanged. `test/layout.test.mjs` renders the player in Chromium at 390 px and asserts the clock's box does not intersect the title's (it needs Playwright, resolved with `require`, so run it with `NODE_PATH` pointing at a `node_modules` that has it; without one it skips and says why).

## 0.11.0 (2026-10-28)

- **The device reads as the Claude app** (Gary, 2026-10-28, watching a case study: "it doesn't look enough like Claude Code. It should look a lot more like Claude Code. Right now, it looks like we're misleading people into thinking that we have a separate app."). The screen inside every device is now the Claude app's Code session, its colours sampled from the iOS app: the night `#151515`, an iOS status bar, a header with the "‹ Code" back label, the session title centred with "MacBook Pro" under it, the activity spinner (turning while work runs) and the "…" button; replies as plain light text with no name label or bubble; the person's turns as dark rounded bubbles; tool calls as compact grey rows (diamond, step, chevron); "1 running task" in blue while work is in flight; a board as the app's Question card (rounded `#20201f` card, the "Question" label, the question, then the chosen answer in brighter text), with the finger's presses and the typed Other answer happening inside that card; and the field at the bottom: rounded dark field, the mic at its left, a stop square while work runs and the round orange send (`#c6613f`), then the home indicator. The MacBook window draws the same conversation as the desktop app, title at the left, on a neutral dark wallpaper instead of the ribbon. The player's own controls moved outside the device: Autoplay sits beside the device switch, and the work counter (and the "Illustrative" note for a recast) sits under that bar. Sizes are in the app's points (`--pt`), so the drawn iPhone scales like the real one. The skin is `CLAUDE_CSS` (exported from `player-css.mjs`), appended after the base sheet and any theme; the screen carries `data-cc="phone"` or `data-cc="desk"`; `StepLine` is exported. Tested in `test/claude-skin.test.mjs`.
- **A hand presses every button the replay presses for the person** (Gary, 2026-10-28: "make it much more clear that there's like a finger pressing buttons. And then when it's a none of the above choice, it should show like a simulation of someone typing out the none of the above choice"). On Autoplay a fingertip comes up from the bottom of the phone, travels to the control (send, a board option, the Other row, the Other field's send), presses it with a ripple, and only then does the choice land; on a MacBook (whole, or the window at phone width) it is the arrow pointer with a click ripple, and it stays on screen between clicks. When the person typed their board answer rather than picking one (`chosen: null` with the text in `answer`), the replay taps Other, the row opens into a field ("Sam is typing their own answer"), the exact recorded text types out with a caret (the first line one character at a time, about 20 a second, the rest in quick frames, so a 300-word answer takes under 4 s), then the finger taps the field's send and the answer lands as before. A viewer playing the person who taps Other sees the same field fill and send, without the finger. With `prefers-reduced-motion` there is no travel: the press shows in place and the answer appears whole. The gestures are pure timelines (`src/gesture.mjs`: `tap`, `typeFrames`, `answerScript`), tested in `test/gesture.test.mjs`; controls the hand can reach carry `data-ss-target`. The 0.3.x `speed` switch stays retired: the timelines take `speed` as a parameter, and no embed switch carries it. `test/fixtures.mjs` gains `boardScenario()`, a board with one picked and one typed answer.
- **The timeline outlines whoever is acting** (Gary, 2026-10-21: "Why is the human part highlighted still when it's the agents turn"). The outline sat on the person's block for the whole turn. It now follows the actor: the person while they give the command, the decision while a board waits on them, the agent while it works, nobody at the close (`timelineActor`).
- **The timeline moves with every step** (Gary, 2026-10-21: "the steps should move every time as well, in the bottom timeline thing"). The bar at the bottom used to change only when a turn handed back to the person, so a turn of a hundred steps sat still the whole time it played. Now the agent's block for the turn in progress fills with the work already played, each step sliding it forward, and a decision's block fills when it is answered; earlier turns are full, later ones empty, and the whole bar is full at the close. `timelineFill(sc, {turnIndex, turnWorkMs, turnAskMs, done})` is exported and tested (`test/timeline.test.mjs`).
- **A forbid entry can be a regex** (`"re:\\bAl\\b"`, the same `re:` prefix a replace's `from` takes). A forbidden word matches where a word starts, which is right for "Christ" in "Christofuturist" and wrong for a short name that starts common words: "Al" fired on every "also" and "Ed" on every "editing", so a recast whose story used those words could pass only by deleting the name from the list, which is the one move the list exists to prevent.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-10)

- The scenario format (`supersuit-simulator/1`) and `validate`.
- `convert`: drafts a scenario from a Claude Code session transcript. Human turns only, interrupts marked as redirects, tool work condensed into steps timed by wall clock, plumbing tools folded into the next step, chapters from the session name, files and links collected as artifacts, credentials and home paths removed with every cut recorded.
- Questions the agent asked are kept as the whole board the person saw (`step.ask = { questions: [{ header, question, multiSelect, options: [{ label, description }], answer, chosen }], dismissed }`), read from the session's structured result, and the player draws them as that board: numbered options, the pick checked, a typed answer shown as the person's own words, a closed board marked.
- Steps and artifacts carry when they happened, so a replay interleaves them with replies.
- The player: `SimulatorPlayer` (`@supersuit/simulator/player`), mobile first, with persona picker, artifacts and timeline switches; `playback` condenses long turns without changing their totals.
- `tidy`: cleans the person's commands for display (typo fixes from a reviewer, screenshot paths to [screenshot]), refusing any fix that rewrites what was said; originals kept in `human.raw`, changes in `cuts`.
- Phone numbers and email addresses are removed from everything a scenario keeps, recorded as cuts.
- Artifacts are deliverables only (pages, media, documents), never code, config or folders; `tidy` takes `artifacts: { drop, add }` so a reviewer can add what the transcript cannot show (kind `other`, e.g. a release submitted to a store).
- `recast`: tells a different story from the same real run (new persona and subject, same commands, steps, timing and decisions), marked illustrative on screen, with a `forbid` list that refuses to finish while any word from the original survives.
- Boards are playable and match the native question board: pinned to the bottom of the phone in place of the composer, one question at a time with a tab per question. Playback pauses on each board, the viewer taps an option (or Other, or Type something else where the person closed it), each pick is answered with the real choice, and the recording resumes on the real path. Autoplay picks the real answers. Once answered, the conversation keeps a compact record (`Asked`): each question, what was chosen, and where the viewer picked differently.
- The mic "says" the command: tapping it streams the words into the composer like dictation (`dictate`), then sends. It is a 48 px circle, not a stretched oval. The counter reads "No work yet" before anything runs.
- Idle time is removed: thinking before a step counts at most 3 min, any one step at most 10 min, a question at most 5 min, and a timed-out command counts as no work. What was removed is kept as `step.idleMs`, recorded in cuts, summed by `summarize`, and named on the end screen.
- Images: what the person pasted is kept from the session (`human.images`); a reviewer attaches what they were shown (`tidy` `images`: on a step, a board, a single option, or the command), since a session often names images only through loop variables; `bundle` copies and shrinks every image into `assets/<id>/` beside the scenario; `recast` must map every image to a replacement by id or it refuses; the player shows option thumbnails, step galleries, pasted images on the command, and tap to enlarge. A step that showed images is never folded into quick steps.
- The close card (`CloseCard`): a celebration when the session ends, in measured numbers only: Freeda's work time and steps against the person's commands and minutes deciding, the chore freed (from a workflow catalog row), the edge points earned with their four counts, the things made, and the idle time left out. Never time saved. The facts live in an optional `close` block (`tidy` sets it; `recast` carries it, recasts its words, and may restate its measured numbers for the turns it keeps); `validate` refuses an edge total that is not the sum of its counts.
- Embeddable in any page or book: an `/embed` page for an `<iframe>` (query switches `scenario`, `artifacts`, `timeline`, `autoplay`, `height`), a `<supersuit-simulator>` custom element (`@supersuit/simulator/embed`), `scenarioId` and `height` props, and every overlay contained inside the player, never fixed to the host page's viewport.
- `bundle` writes a provenance recipe beside every image (mode derive: what it came from, what resized it, and the source's own recipe nested), so a bundled scenario passes a wiki's image-provenance gate.
- `recast` gives every image a neutral id (`<recast id>-image-<n>`, one per picture), since ids became file names and carried the original's subject.
- A session opening with a slash command shows the command as typed, not its tags; usage-limit and API-error notices are dropped (they are the harness, and carry the operator's plan and time zone); recast checks and recasts the close card's source notes.
- The `simulator` CLI: `convert`, `validate`, `tidy`, `recast`, `bundle`, `serve`.
